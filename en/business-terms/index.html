Business terms

These terms govern the relationship between Iván Hijano Pérez ("Klendar") and the business that registers on the Platform ("the Business"). By submitting the registration, the person submitting it declares they are authorised to bind the Business.

1. Registration and verification

  • The Business provides accurate details (name, category, address, tax ID, contact). Klendar verifies the registration before making it public and may reject it with reasons.
  • The Business designates an account owner and may add managers and staff. The owner is responsible for the actions of their team.
  • To register a business and to own it (including when receiving it in a handover) you must be 18 or over. To be part of its team you must be 16 or over, and 18 if the business is marked "18+". Klendar checks this with the date of birth in the profile, and a business cannot be marked "18+" while anyone on its team is under 18.
  • If the Business significantly changes its name, or changes its city or address (by more than 1 km), Klendar may review it again. In the meantime the business page stays published and verified, and what the Business has published and still running moves to the new address. If the review shows that the registration conditions are no longer met, Klendar may act under section 6.
  • Claiming a business. If someone else created a business's page, whoever runs the business can ask for ownership from the page itself (“Is this your business?”): with an account, aged 18 or over, giving their role, a business phone number or email and evidence (a document, a photo or an explanation that can be checked). Klendar reviews it by hand and, if it approves it, hands ownership over to them; whoever was the owner is notified with the reason, may stay on the team if Klendar considers it appropriate and may ask for the decision to be reviewed within 6 months by writing to info@klendar.app. Claiming with false information may lead to the account being suspended.
  • Duplicate pages. A business that is already on Klendar cannot be registered again: if it is theirs, the Business must claim the existing page. If Klendar detects two pages for the same business (the same name in the same city, or almost the same address), it may merge them into one: the one that goes is deleted, the people who had it in their favourites and its links move to the one that stays, and its owner is notified. If both have content (publications, reviews, stamp cards…), Klendar sorts it out with the owners before merging them.

2. Service

Klendar lets the Business publish flash deals and events (with place reservations and a waiting list if it turns them on, and in series of dates that people can follow), news, its menu and opening hours, describe what its place is like (“About your place”), manage its page, offer stamp cards and a birthday gift, message its customers (at most once a week per venue, reviewed by Klendar), work with promoters with their own links, lists and offers, validate redemptions via QR and view statistics. People can share its publications and page outside Klendar (a link and a stories image). Klendar does not take part in the sale and does not charge users.

2a. How what people see is ordered

In Discover, Explore, the map and the agenda, publications are ordered using these criteria, in this order of importance: (1) in Discover and the agenda, paid featured placements (“Featured”), which always carry that label, and in the collections Klendar puts together (for example, “With children”), the order Klendar gives them; (2) with “Based on the weather” on (it is by default), if it's raining today, today's indoor plans first and today's outdoor plans last, and if the weather is nice, today's terrace or outdoor plans first, based on what the Business ticks in “About your place” or in each publication; (3) the order the person chooses in “Sort” (nearest, soonest or newest); (4) if the person has chosen their interests (3 to 5 categories), publications in those categories weigh more within the order they've chosen, without hiding anything else: with “Newest” they count as if they were two days newer, with “Soonest” as if they started six hours earlier and with “Nearest” as if they were half as far away; (5) events Klendar imports from councils' public listings (open data, shown with their source) appear at most one in every five, interleaved in the same order, as long as there are publications from businesses and organisations (on the map, at most a quarter of the points; in collections Klendar orders by hand, their order applies); nothing is hidden. What an organisation publishes itself counts like any other publication. The person's filters (distance, category, price, “When?”, “The place”, “Open now”, only with a discount) decide what is shown, not the order. “Trending” is a label (many different people got it today) and doesn't change the order. The Business's rating, its fee or its number of publications don't affect the order. If there's nothing nearby, publications from outside the radius are suggested based on the person's interests, searches and the categories they look at, stored on their phone or in their account.

3. Obligations of the Business

  • Honour published deals under the stated conditions for as long as they are valid and until the declared capacity runs out.
  • Honour the rewards on its stamp cards and the birthday gift for as long as it offers them.
  • Publish the final price the person will pay, including VAT and any other charges.
  • If it announces a price reduction, give as the previous price the lowest price it applied in the previous 30 days.
  • Publish only accurate, lawful content it holds the rights to (photos, text), in line with the Community guidelines.
  • Comply with the rules applicable to its activity, including those on advertising alcoholic beverages (Spanish Law 34/1988 and regional regulations) and the protection of minors: nightlife venues, or deals that include alcohol, must be marked "18+".
  • Describe its place and each publication truthfully in “About your place” and in “For children?” / “Indoor or outdoors?”: in particular “Wheelchair accessible”, “Child-friendly” and “Play area”, which people rely on to decide.
  • If it works with promoters: choose them, add and remove them in Klendar and answer for what they do on its behalf; agree and pay whatever they are owed outside Klendar; use the data on their lists only to manage entry that night and their figures, and not invite anyone under 16 as a promoter (or under 18 if the Business is "18+"). Klendar is not a party to the relationship between the Business and its promoters.
  • Honour promoter offers with the places and time limit it has set, like any other offer.
  • Respect the dates it publishes in a series; if it cancels a date with bookings, Klendar notifies each person and cancels their bookings.
  • Use the user data it learns (name and photo when they get a code or book, the attendee list and its promoters' lists) only to provide the service, with no purposes of its own and no disclosure.
  • If a publication asks for a student card, a youth card or being over 65, check it itself. Klendar doesn't verify anyone's identity or age for these discounts; the scanner only reminds it to ask.
  • If it shows Klendar on a TV at its venue (“Show on TV”), be responsible for where it is and who sees it, and unlink it from “Show on TV” if it moves it, sells it or it stops being its own.
  • If it marks that it's showing a match or an event (“We're showing it”), hold the rights to show it at its venue (for example, the hospitality subscription of the platform that broadcasts it) and really show it. Klendar doesn't check this and may remove the mark if it receives complaints.
  • No fixed venue. If it ticks “I don't have a fixed venue”, every publication must say where it is (name and address of the place) and it must have the place's permission to advertise it. It is responsible to people for the event as its organiser (price, capacity, safety, and the licences and insurance required by its region's public events rules), even if the venue belongs to someone else.
  • Who's performing. If it adds artists to an event, they must really be performing, under their stage name as it is. Klendar creates a public artist page with that name showing their upcoming dates at any venue; the Business only adds the name: Klendar reviews and decides the photo, description and links. It mustn't add a private individual as an artist, or offensive or misleading names (advertising someone who isn't coming).
  • Allergens and diets. If it ticks “Gluten-free options”, “Coeliac-friendly”, “Vegan options”, “Vegetarian options”, “Lactose-free options” or “Halal” on its page, it declares it under its own responsibility: it must only tick what it always offers and can guarantee. “Gluten-free” is a regulated claim (Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) No 828/2014) and the allergen information for what it serves is its own (Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011 and Spanish Royal Decree 126/2015); what it puts on Klendar doesn't replace it: it must also have it at its venue. Klendar doesn't check these marks and may remove them if it receives well-founded complaints.
  • Links and menu. The links it adds to its page (“Book”, “Order delivery”, “Order at the venue” or others) must lead to its own pages or to services it really uses for its business, without misleading people about where they go or asking for data or payments under another identity. What happens on those pages (bookings, orders, payments, their terms and privacy) is a matter between the Business and that service, not Klendar. Its menu files (photos and PDFs) must be its menu, with up-to-date prices and allergens, and no personal data. Klendar may remove a link or file that doesn't comply.
  • Coupons and gifts. If it turns on the next-visit coupon or sends a gift to its regulars, it commits to giving it as described while the code is valid, just like an offer. If it leaves Klendar or its page is deactivated, unused ones are cancelled and Klendar lets each person know.
  • First visit. A “first-time visitors only” offer must really be one: it mustn't use it to draw people in with something it then doesn't give. Klendar checks that the person hasn't redeemed at the Business before; its team can't use it.
  • Regulars. The list of its most loyal customers is only for thanking them within Klendar. It mustn't copy it, combine it with other data or use it to contact people in other ways.

3a. Organisation accounts

A council, a municipal district council, a traders' association, a residents' association, an NGO or another non-profit organisation (the “Organisation”) can have an organisation account. It is requested when signing up to Klendar (“A council, association or NGO”) or, if it already has a page, from “Your page › Organisation account”, with the Organisation's official name, tax ID (CIF) and official website, the name and role of the person asking and a document proving that they represent it. Klendar grants it after checking (see “How we check”). An Organisation that signs up this way cannot publish until Klendar has checked it. With the organisation account, the page and publications carry a badge with the type of organisation (“Council”, “Traders' association”…) and appear under the “Public listings” filter.

An Organisation publishes like any Business and also undertakes to: a) publish only activities open to the public or of interest to the neighbourhood or city (festivities, street markets, local shopping campaigns, cultural, sports or charity activities); b) not use the account for electoral or party propaganda, nor for institutional advertising that is prohibited during election periods; c) keep up to date who manages the account and let us know if they no longer represent the Organisation.

How we check: we review the document (for example, the appointment, a certificate from the secretary bearing the seal or secure verification code, the board minutes or the entry in the relevant register of associations or entities) and compare it with the Organisation's public information: its official website, its e-office or the relevant register. We may ask for more information, write to the email address or call the phone number shown on the Organisation's official website (never one given by the applicant), refuse the account giving the reason, and remove the badge if the details stop being true, without prejudice to section 6. Requesting an organisation account with false details or documents may lead to the account being suspended.

Until Klendar says otherwise, organisation accounts pay no fee. If that changes, we will give at least 15 days' notice and the Organisation may leave at no cost.

3b. Passes

The Business can sell passes for its venue and use Klendar to keep count of the uses. When it does:

  • The Business sells the pass and takes payment for it itself, at its venue and by its own means. Klendar doesn't charge the person anything, doesn't receive that money and isn't a party to the contract between the Business and them. The price the Business enters in Klendar is for information (for its stats and so the person knows what they bought) and must be the price it charges.
  • The Business must honour every pass sold on its terms (uses, what it includes, expiry and conditions) even if it later pauses, removes or changes it: changes only apply to later sales. The Business is responsible to the person for the service, refunds and claims, under consumer law.
  • Conditions and expiry must be clear and lawful. The Business can't set conditions that consumer law treats as unfair (for example, a disproportionate expiry or losing what was paid if the Business closes).
  • The Business only adds a pass once the person has paid for it, and only records a use when they enjoy it. Every sale and use is recorded with who on the team did it, and the person sees it in their history. The Business is responsible for what its team does.
  • The tax obligations of the sale (invoice or receipt and VAT) are the Business's.
  • If the Business is going to close, stop providing the service or delete its Klendar account with unused passes, it must tell those people in good time and honour or refund what is due. After it leaves, the person keeps their history on Klendar, but the Business's obligation remains: Klendar doesn't take it on.

3c. Virtual queue

It's optional. If the Business opens it, it will call people in order of arrival, won't remove anyone without reason and will give a reasonable time to turn up. Anyone it adds by hand (without the app), with their permission and only with the name it needs to call them. It is responsible for what its team does with the queue; Klendar may turn it off for a business that misuses it, under section 6.

3d. Groups and companies

If the Business ticks “We take groups”, people can ask it for a quote for groups. When it does:

  • What it says (up to how many people, what for, its note and each proposal) must be true and achievable. The price per person in a proposal is indicative: if it changes, it must tell the person before confirming anything.
  • It must reply within the time limit (72 hours or until the day of the plan, whichever comes first; it sees it on each request) or say it can't. Requests it doesn't answer expire on their own.
  • The contact details the person gives it when accepting its proposal are only for confirming that plan with them. It can't use them for advertising, pass them on or keep them longer than necessary; from the moment it receives them it is responsible for them (GDPR). Klendar deletes them from its dashboard 30 days after the date.
  • The Business agrees the booking, payment, deposits and cancellations with the person, outside Klendar, and is responsible for them under consumer law. Klendar isn't a party to that agreement.
  • Only the owner and managers see and reply to requests.

3e. Giveaways

The Business can run giveaways on Klendar. When it creates one the Business is the organiser (with the company name and tax ID it declares, which appear in the rules) and accepts:

  • Free, no purchase. Entering can't require buying, paying or anything other than what Klendar allows (having the business in favourites). It can't ask for different conditions outside Klendar (sharing, tagging, buying).
  • Rules. Klendar generates them from its details and publishes them before it starts. Its conditions (the “Conditions” field) can't contradict or narrow them. Once someone has entered, the giveaway can't be changed; it can only be cancelled for a justified reason, with the reason, which is sent to the people who entered. Cancelling without cause or repeatedly is considered misleading.
  • The prize. It must exist, be what it describes and have the approximate value it gives. The Business hands it over, free of charge, to the winner (or the reserve whose turn it is) within a reasonable time and as the rules or its conditions say, and marks it in Klendar when it hands it over. It can't swap it for something of lower value or charge anything to hand it over. Tobacco, vaping products and betting can't be given away; a prize with alcohol is for over-18s only.
  • Tax. If the prize is worth more than €300, the Business makes the 19% income tax withholding or payment on account and reports it (forms 111 and 190, or as applicable), and asks the winner for the details it needs for it. If its autonomous community's rules require any formality for promotional giveaways, that is also up to it. Klendar doesn't withhold or report anything.
  • Data. It only receives the winner's name and photo (and the tax details they give it). It uses them only to hand over the prize and comply with the tax authority, and is responsible for them (GDPR).
  • Verifiable. Klendar runs the draw automatically and verifiably; the Business can't choose the winner or ask for it to be redrawn.
  • Up to 3 giveaways open at once. Klendar may remove a giveaway that breaches this, the guidelines or the law, telling the people who entered.

4. Free trial, pricing and invoicing

  • Free trial. On registration, the Business gets a 30-day free trial with every feature, with no card and no automatic renewal. It can choose how to pay before it ends: nothing is charged until the day after the last day of the trial. If, when the trial ends, it hasn't chosen how to pay, the Business is blocked (section 4a). We remind it 7 days and 1 day before.
  • Launch, free. While Klendar is starting in a city, we may give its businesses more free time (“Launch, free”). We tell the Business the day it ends at least one month in advance (by email, with a notice in the app and in “My business”) and remind it 7 days and 1 day before; from the following day, as when the trial ends, it needs to choose how to pay or the Business is blocked (section 4a). As long as it has no end date, it doesn't end.
  • Price. After that, the service is provided under a single plan: a flat fee of €19.90 a month per venue (or €199 a year), with no limit on publications or redemptions. With several venues, the first one pays the full fee and, from the second one on, the fee for each venue is lower, according to the table at klendar.app/en/pricing. Prices are excluding VAT: the applicable VAT is added (currently 21%). If the paying venue is in the Canary Islands, Ceuta or Melilla (we go by the postcode of its address on Klendar), the transaction is not subject to Spanish VAT and the invoice says so; if the billing address the Business gives doesn't match the venue's, we check it with the Business. Klendar charges no commission on redemptions or on the Business's sales.
  • Klendar will notify the Business at least one month in advance before any price change. If the Business does not agree, it may cancel before the change applies, without penalty.
  • Payment is currently made by bank transfer: the Business requests it at info@klendar.app and Klendar sends the details. If other methods (card or direct debit) are offered later, they will be announced beforehand. Klendar issues an invoice for each payment.

4a. Outstanding payments and blocking

When it's blocked. The Business is blocked if its trial, “Launch, free” period or paid period ends (including after cancelling the subscription) and there is no other payment in force, or if a payment stays outstanding for more than 7 days from the first failed charge: a declined card, a direct debit that can't be collected or a direct debit returned by the bank, even weeks later, or an agreed bank transfer that doesn't arrive. We remind it by email, in the app and in “My business” 7 days and 1 day before and on the day it's blocked, with the reason and how to pay. A direct debit payment still being processed doesn't block. For card or direct debit payments, Stripe retries the charge during those days and sends an email.

What it means. While blocked, its publications are hidden, its page shows “Not available right now” and nobody on its team can publish, edit the business page or use Loyalty, promoters or other tools. Nothing is deleted. At any time it can:

  • pay (in “Your plan” in the web dashboard) and, once the payment goes through, everything works again on its own;
  • download its data and leave Klendar;
  • validate at the door the codes, bookings and passes people already have, because what has already been given is honoured: passes sold, stamps, rewards and gifts, codes and open giveaways. It must honour them in the meantime.

Other measures. If the debt remains, we may cancel the subscription and claim what is owed. Returning a direct debit or charging back a service that was provided counts as non-payment. If the owner doesn't use Klendar for a long time, the inactive-account rules apply (notice at 24 months; a business is never deleted without Klendar deciding so).

5. Term, pausing, handover and termination

The plan is monthly (or yearly, if chosen) and renews automatically, with no minimum term.

  • Cancelling the subscription. The Business may cancel it at any time, without penalty, from the dashboard (“Leave Klendar”, in the app or at klendar.app/panel) or by writing to info@klendar.app. Cancellation takes effect at the end of the period already paid (or of the free trial): until then everything carries on as before and no later period is charged. Klendar confirms it by email and the Business can undo it before that date. Cancelling does not delete the business page, publications or data. When it ends, if there's no other payment, the Business is blocked (section 4a) until it pays again; nothing is deleted. If it cancels while paused, see “Seasonal pause”.
  • Seasonal pause. If the Business closes for a while (holidays, works, low season), the owner can pause the plan from “Your plan” (app or dashboard), choosing the return date or “no date”, which is at most 6 months per pause. While it lasts:
    • nothing is charged and the days already paid for and not used are kept (for example, 12 days of the month or 200 of the year): when it comes back they're used first and after that the plan is charged as usual. Nothing is lost or refunded. With payment by bank transfer, the end date of what was paid is extended by the length of the pause;
    • the Business is stopped just as if it were blocked (section 4a): its publications are hidden, its page says “Temporarily closed until [date]” and its team can't publish, edit or use the tools. As when blocked, it can validate at the door what people already have, download its data and leave Klendar, and also “Reopen” whenever it likes;
    • it comes back on its own on the chosen day (we remind it 7 days before) or sooner with “Reopen”. When it comes back we let the people who have it in their favourites know. If the charge after that fails, section 4a applies.
    It can't be paused with a payment outstanding or being processed. If the subscription covers several venues, they're all paused. If it cancels while paused, it comes back, the days already paid for are used and the subscription ends then. “Closing until further notice” hides the Business but doesn't pause billing.
  • Closing until further notice. The Business can stop being shown whenever it wants: its page and publications stop appearing on the Platform (anyone with the link will see “Temporarily closed”) until it reopens from the dashboard. Closing doesn't cancel or pause the subscription (to stop paying, it has to be paused or cancelled) and does not cancel bookings and codes already issued, which the Business must honour or cancel from the dashboard, which tells each person.
  • Handover. The owner can hand the Business over to an adult member of its team, who must accept it (the offer expires after 14 days). Once accepted, that person is the holder for the purposes of these terms. What has already been paid stays with the Business until the end of the period; from then on, the new holder chooses how to pay (the previous holder's subscription stops covering it and they're no longer charged for it). Klendar takes no part in any underlying transaction (sale, transfer…).
  • Deleting the Business. The owner can delete it from the dashboard after confirming their identity, and can first download its data (business page, publications with their figures, report and stamp cards without customer data). When it is deleted, Klendar cancels unused bookings and codes and tells each person affected (including anyone with pending stamps, rewards or gifts) and the team; the business page, publications, reviews, stamp cards and team are permanently deleted. Amounts already paid are not refunded. The subscription is cancelled at that moment (if it covers other venues, it stops covering this one). Billing data is kept for the statutory period (tax 4 years, commercial 6 years). A personal account cannot be deleted while it owns a business.

Klendar may terminate the contract for serious breach (not honouring deals, unlawful content, redemption fraud) with a reasoned notice.

6. Moderation, suspension and appeal

Klendar may remove content or temporarily suspend the profile in response to substantiated reports or breaches. The Business will receive the reasons and may ask for the decision to be reviewed within 6 months by writing to info@klendar.app (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065). The Business's text goes through automatic moderation that may hold it for review until someone at Klendar has reviewed it (see “How we moderate” in the Community guidelines); the business page and menu stay visible in the meantime. Blocking for non-payment (section 4a) is notified in advance with its reasons in the notice itself; it's lifted automatically on payment.

If Klendar takes down a publication, its unused bookings and codes are cancelled. If it deactivates the Business's page, the unused bookings and codes of all its publications, unredeemed stamp-card rewards (each person gets their stamps back) and pending birthday gifts are cancelled. In both cases Klendar tells each person affected with a generic reason, without mentioning reports or who made them.

If the owner's account is suspended, Klendar pauses their businesses ("Closed until further notice"): they stop being shown and their team is told, but bookings and codes already issued remain valid and the rest of the team can validate them. A suspended account cannot publish, change the business page, validate codes, invite people or join a team. When the suspension is lifted, Klendar decides whether to reopen the business or let the owner reopen it.

7. Intellectual property and data

The Business keeps ownership of its content and grants Klendar a licence to display it on the Platform, include it in the stories images and links that people share outside Klendar, and use it in Klendar's promotional materials (with the option to object to the latter). Klendar and the Business act as independent controllers with regard to the personal data each of them processes.

8. Liability

Klendar is responsible for diligently providing the technical service. It is not liable for the Business's activity or for users' claims arising from deals not honoured, which are borne by the Business. Klendar's maximum liability is limited to the fees paid in the preceding 12 months, except in cases of wilful misconduct or gross negligence.

9. Governing law and jurisdiction

Spanish law. For businesses, the parties submit to the courts of [Full address, Spain].

10. Changes

Klendar may change these terms by notifying the Business at least 15 days in advance (one month if the price changes) by email and in the dashboard. If the Business does not agree, it may cancel before they apply, without penalty.

Each version of these terms shows its publication date and, if it does not apply that same day, the date from which it applies, and its changes are listed in the history at the bottom of this page. The notice is sent to the email address of the Business owner and stays in “My business” and in the dashboard until it is marked as read or, at most, until 30 days after it applies. Klendar keeps a record of the version the Business accepted when it signed up and of when it was notified of each change. If the Business keeps using Klendar from the date a change applies, the new version also applies to it.
